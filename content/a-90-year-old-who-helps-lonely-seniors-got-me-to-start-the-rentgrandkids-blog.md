> **TL;DR:** For about two months I put off the part of rentgrandkids.org I dreaded: telling people about it and starting a blog. Then I read about Ratnam Periowsamy, 90, a widow, mother of five and grandmother of six, who walks her Singapore neighborhood with a stick to sit with lonely seniors. If she's still giving back at 90, how much should I be doing? So I wrote this: the first post for rentgrandkids, where seniors and kids help each other. Age is just a number.

This is the first post on the rentgrandkids blog. It's for anyone who wants seniors and kids to know each other, and especially for older people who feel like nobody needs them anymore. It's not a guide to professional care or a replacement for family. Most stories about inspiring seniors treat them as people to be helped. I think seniors and kids can help each other, and both come out ahead. It starts with a 90-year-old woman I've never met.

## She sits down next to them

In the mornings, a 90-year-old woman named Ratnam Periowsamy leaves her home in Toa Payoh, Singapore, and walks the blocks of flats with her stick. She's looking for older people who are sitting alone.

When she finds one, she sits down next to them and asks what's going on. A lot of them don't want to talk. So she comes back. And comes back again. As [Upworthy reported](https://scoop.upworthy.com/90yo-ratnam-walks-neighborhood-to-help-lonely-seniors-g), she put it simply: "The third time, they're sure to talk already."

The same Upworthy piece describes her as a volunteer with Care Corner Singapore's Project Neighbour Network, where she also helps train others to befriend seniors. The article mentions she had a fall in late 2023. She kept going anyway, with the stick.

I read that and felt inspired.

## How much should I be doing?

Up to that point, I'd been stuck. I'm working on getting rentgrandkids.org live, and for two months I'd put off telling people about it: socializing it, starting a blog. It was writer's block. I hadn't found a tool to get past it.

What stuck with me in Ratnam's story wasn't a number about loneliness. It was who she is. A widow. A mother of five. A grandmother of six. Ninety years old. And she still wants to give back to her community.

So I asked myself: if she's still doing this at 90, how much should I be doing? If you've been putting something off, it's worth asking yourself the same thing.

That's when I sat down to do the work I'd been putting off.

## What people get wrong about getting old

I've heard the idea that people get grumpier and shut themselves off as they age. Ratnam doesn't fit it, and she inspires me. My view, not a research finding, is that when older people end up alone, circumstance like health often matters more than character.

I believe there's always a way to be productive and make the world a little better every day, and that a lot of what we think age stops us from doing is in our heads. That's what I'm trying to do with rentgrandkids.

## "Fine, but she's one in a million"

The fair pushback: Ratnam is rare. Most seniors deal with health problems, money problems or loneliness, so "age is just a number" is an easy thing to say.

Those limits are real. A [University of Michigan poll](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/1-3-older-adults-still-experience-loneliness-and-isolation) found that in 2024, 33% of Americans aged 50 to 80 felt lonely some of the time or often. The same researchers found loneliness was higher among people in fair or poor health.

But I don't read her story as "be a superhero at 90." I read it as one example of someone finding a place to plug in. For her, that place was Care Corner.

rentgrandkids is meant to be that kind of place, and it's free. It won't replace family or professional care; it's a way for seniors and kids to get to know each other. If you're a senior, it's a way to find purpose by helping kids. If you're young, it's a chance to help an elder. I hope both sides get something out of it. For seniors, that could be company and a hand with physical tasks. For young people, it could be new connections and a chance to learn from experience, give something back, and spend time away from their screens. The point is to build real relationships between the two generations. Many seniors and young people feel isolated, and I believe a regular connection between them can help both sides.

## One thing you can do this week

Ratnam's walking stick is real. So was her fall. She still goes out in the mornings and sits down next to someone who's alone.

I'm a lot younger than 90, and it took me two months to write one post. What got me past the block was Galascribe, which I have no connection to beyond using it: it interviewed me about all this and turned my answers into a draft. Now it's written.

You don't need a walking stick or a website to start. Find one person near you who's alone and sit with them once this week. And if you know a senior who wants a purpose or a young person who wants to help an elder, share this post with them and take a look at rentgrandkids.org.
